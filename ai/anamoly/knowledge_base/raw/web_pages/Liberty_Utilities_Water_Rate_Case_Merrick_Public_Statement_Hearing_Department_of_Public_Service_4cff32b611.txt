Case Information
Case 26-W-0358
-  Proceeding on Motion of the Commission as to the Rates, Charges, Rules and Regulations of Liberty Utilities (New York Water) Corp. for Water Service.
Public comment is invited concerning proposed changes in the water delivery rates and practices of Liberty Utilities (New York Water) Corp. (the Company). The Company filed amendments to its water tariff schedule on May 29, 2026, proposing to increase its annual water delivery revenues for the 12-month period ending April 30, 2028. The Company initially proposed to increase its annual revenues by approximately $24.59 million (or 21.87 percent) for its Service Area (SA) 1, and $13.52 million (or 30.14 percent) for SA 2. The Company estimated that the requested increase in revenues would result in an average monthly residential bill increase of approximately $27.77, or 28.3 percent, for customers in SA 1 outside of the New Vernon district; and $35.47, or 59.35 percent, for SA 1 customers in New Vernon. For SA 2, the increase would result in average monthly residential bill increases of $26.89, or 29.71 percent, in the Sea Cliff district, and $21.20, or 36.99 percent, in the Merrick district. The residential bill impacts are based on monthly usage of 8,000 gallons of water per month.
The Company states that its proposed water delivery revenue increases would support capital investments to improve reliability and customer service, and ensure continued provision of safe, reliable, sustainable, and affordable service for its customers. The Company indicates that the primary drivers of the requested revenue increases are capital investments to address aging infrastructure, increase capacity, and comply with federal drinking water standards for PFAS/PFOA substances. Additional drivers include needed resiliency improvements and lead service line replacement, information technology upgrades, other incremental operations and maintenance expenses, depreciation expenses, and taxes.
The Company’s pre-filed testimony and exhibits in this proceeding may be reviewed online at the Department of Public Service web page, www.dps.ny.gov, in
Case 26-W-0358
.
Under New York State law, the Public Service Commission (Commission) must consider a utility's rate proposal and may adopt or reject it, in whole or in part, or modify it.  In doing so, the Commission will consider changes proposed by the participating parties and the public.  Administrative Law Judges (ALJs) are presiding over the gathering of public comments and all evidence relating to the proposals.
