Mayor Mamdani Demands ICE Stop Unauthorized Use of City Lots, Threatens Legal Action
November 2, 2026
What you should know
After ICE agents were reportedly seen staging in City-owned lots, Mayor warns DHS the City will take legal action if ICE continues its unauthorized use of City property
NEW YORK –
Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani today demanded that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) immediately stop its unauthorized and therefore illegal use of New York City-owned property to stage immigration enforcement operations, warning that the City will take legal action if agents continue.
In a letter to Department of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, Acting ICE Director David J. Venturella, and ICE New York Field Office Director Kenneth Genalo, the Mayor made clear that the City has not authorized ICE to use its property for immigration-related operations. He called on ICE to issue guidance explicitly prohibiting agents from using City-owned lots to stage or conduct those operations.
ICE agents have reportedly parked vehicles and held meetings in several unstaffed City-owned lots, including a lot at Van Cortlandt Park in the Bronx and lots along 111th Street on the west side of Flushing Meadows Corona Park in Queens. Those Queens lots sit next to a City-run preschool and several cultural institutions that welcome hundreds of children and families every day.
To further safeguard against ICE’s unauthorized use of City property and lots, as part of Executive Order 13, the Mamdani administration has been working with City agencies to update their policies and protocols related to non-local law enforcement attempts to gain access to City property and lots. Alongside issuing the letter to ICE, the administration is also announcing a new NYC Parks policy today for frontline workers, which strengthens the procedures that Parks employees must follow when responding to attempts by non-local law enforcement, including ICE, to access non-public and public areas of Parks property. This new policy and ongoing training will ensure Parks employees are equipped to enforce our local laws and protect Parks spaces for the safe use and enjoyment of all New Yorkers.

Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani.

Earlier this year, Governor Hochul signed the Local Cops, Local Crimes Act in law, which prohibits state and local law enforcement agencies from entering into agreements to carry out federal civil immigration enforcement, ensuring local law enforcement resources remain focused on protecting New Yorkers rather than carrying out federal immigration operations.

Agents had reportedly been staging operations from a private lot a few blocks away.
